<QuickAnswer>
Most Texas drivers get a ticket dismissed by taking a 6-hour defensive driving course. Ask the court for it by the answer date on your ticket and plead no contest, then hand in your certificate, your driving record and a short signed statement within 90 days. It costs about $175 in all. If the course isn't open to you, the judge may offer deferred disposition instead: the case goes on hold and is dismissed if you meet the judge's conditions.
</QuickAnswer>

Texas has two routes to a dismissed ticket, and people mix them up. The course is the one most drivers use, because if you qualify the judge has to allow it. Deferred disposition is up to the judge.

## The driving safety course dismissal

Ask for the course, take it, hand in the paperwork, and the ticket is dismissed. No conviction goes on your record, and Texas no longer uses driver points, so the dismissal itself is the win. The charge can't be part of your driving record or used against you, and your insurer can't cancel your policy or raise your premium because you took the course (art. 45A.357).

## Who qualifies

You can use the course if:

- you have a Texas driver's license or permit
- you were going less than 25 mph over the limit, and under 95 mph
- you haven't completed a course in the 12 months before the ticket
- you ask the court by the answer date on your citation, and plead no contest or guilty
- you can show proof of insurance

A few drivers are outside that:

- **You hold a CDL**, even if you were in your own car: the course isn't available to you, and nor is deferred disposition (art. 45A.351(c)). Talk to a traffic lawyer.
- **You don't have a Texas license**: you only qualify if you're on active military duty, or the spouse or child of someone who is. Our [out-of-state ticket guide](/out-of-state-ticket) covers the rest.
- **Passing a stopped school bus, a work-zone ticket or leaving the scene of a crash** aren't covered.
- **Missed the answer date, or used a course in the last year?** The judge can still allow it, but may charge a fine.

## How to do it

### Step 1: Ask the court

By the answer date on your citation, ask the court for the driving safety course and plead no contest. You can ask in person, through a lawyer, or by certified mail postmarked by the answer date. Some courts also take requests by email or through an online portal. The court can charge up to $10 for the request, on top of court costs.

### Step 2: Take a TDLR-approved course

The course has to be approved by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR). Every approved course takes 6 hours, so none is faster than another. We compare them on the [Texas page](/texas).

### Step 3: Order your driving record

Most courts want a certified Type 3A record from the Texas Department of Public Safety (DPS). It's about $12 online and you can print it straight away ([order it from DPS](https://www.dps.texas.gov/section/driver-license/how-order-driver-record)). Some courts pull it themselves and charge you the fee instead. **Don't skip this step.** Without the record, the court sends your paperwork back.

### Step 4: Hand everything in within 90 days

The court gives you 90 days. Your court's paperwork says when the clock starts. Send:

- the certificate of completion from your course
- your driving record, unless the court pulls it
- a short signed statement that you weren't already taking a course and haven't taken one in the past 12 months

Most courts want it by mail or in person. Some take email.

### Step 5: Check it's done

Allow 2 to 4 weeks, then check your DPS driving record online.

## Deferred disposition

This is the judge's option, not your right. You plead no contest or guilty and pay the court costs, and the judge puts the case on hold for up to 180 days. Meet the conditions and the case is dismissed, with nothing reported to DPS. The judge can add a fee of up to the fine and other conditions, such as a course. Drivers under 25 with a moving violation always have to take one.

Ask for it when the course is off the table: 25 mph or more over, or a course used in the past year.

## If you miss the deadline

The dismissal falls away and the court can convict you on the original ticket, which then goes on your record. Call the court before the deadline if you're running late. The court can give you more time if you have a good reason.

## What it costs

| Item | Cost |
|------|------|
| Court costs and state fines | $134 |
| Court fee for the course request | up to $10 |
| Defensive driving course | from $25 |
| Type 3A driving record | about $12 |
| **Total** | **about $170 to $180** |

You'd owe the $134 in court costs and state fines even if you just paid the ticket. So the course adds roughly $45, and it saves you the fine and the conviction. A conviction would cost you about $4,824 in higher insurance over three years (an estimate; your insurer's figure will differ). For more on that comparison, see [traffic school vs paying the ticket](/blog/traffic-school-vs-paying-ticket).

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use the course more than once?**
Yes, but only once every 12 months.

**What if I already paid my ticket?**
Paying usually means you've pleaded and been convicted, so the course is off the table. Call the court straight away; some allow a short window to change course.

**Does every Texas court have to allow it?**
Yes, if you qualify and ask in time. It's a statewide right, and a court can't refuse an eligible request.

**Is defensive driving the same as traffic school?**
In [Texas](/texas), yes. "Defensive driving", "traffic school" and "driving safety course" all mean the same TDLR-approved course. For other states, see [how to dismiss a traffic ticket online](/blog/how-to-dismiss-traffic-ticket-online).
