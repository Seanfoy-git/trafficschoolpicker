<QuickAnswer>
Whether traffic school removes points depends entirely on your state. Some states fully dismiss the ticket (Texas, Arizona). Some mask it so insurers can't see it (California, Nevada). Some reduce your active point total (New York, Georgia, Virginia). Some do not remove points at all.
</QuickAnswer>

The phrase "remove points" means different things in different states, which is why drivers get confused. This guide maps exactly what happens in every major state when you complete a traffic school course. For a step-by-step walkthrough of the full process, see [how to dismiss a traffic ticket online](/blog/how-to-dismiss-traffic-ticket-online).

One assumption runs underneath everything below: that your license was issued by the same state where you got the ticket. If it wasn't — if you were [ticketed in a state you aren't licensed in](/out-of-state-ticket) — the math changes completely, because a course that only credits points has nothing to work on when the record those points would land on lives in another state.

## The three types of outcomes

### 1. Full dismissal

The ticket is removed entirely from your public driving record. No points are assessed. Your insurance company sees nothing.

**States with full statewide dismissal:** [Texas](/texas), [Arizona](/arizona).

**Court-by-court dismissal (no statewide program, decided by the court on your citation):** Oklahoma, [Kansas](/kansas), New Mexico, and others.

In Texas, this happens through a driving safety course (DSC) dismissal under Code of Criminal Procedure art. 45A.352, available once every 12 months. In Arizona, through the Defensive Driving School (DDS) program. The conviction never appears on your record.

### 2. Masking (confidential)

The ticket is not erased, but it is hidden from your insurer and from the public. It remains on your internal DMV record. This achieves the same practical effect as dismissal for insurance purposes — your rates do not increase.

**States with masking:** [California](/california), Nevada, [Florida](/florida).

[California](/california) is the most important example. Completing a DMV-licensed traffic school in California means the conviction is "held confidential." Your insurance company cannot legally see it. Your rates cannot increase because of it. But it does exist on your record internally.

### 3. Point reduction

The underlying ticket and conviction remain on your public record, but your active point total is reduced. This helps prevent license suspension (which is triggered by accumulating too many points) and may qualify you for an insurance discount, but it does not undo the ticket itself.

**States with point reduction only:** [New York](/new-york) (up to 4 points via PIRP), [Georgia](/georgia) (up to 7 points, classroom DDS course), [Virginia](/virginia) (5 safe-driving points via DIC), [Ohio](/ohio) (2 points via DSP), [Wisconsin](/wisconsin) (3 demerit points, once every 3 years, via WisDOT courses), [Tennessee](/tennessee) (points removed via a 4-hour driver-improvement course, once every 4 years), Nebraska (2 points), Indiana (4-point credit).

## Does "masking" actually protect you from insurance increases?

Yes, in states that use masking. Insurance companies are legally prohibited from using a masked conviction to increase your premium in [California](/california). They cannot see the conviction through standard driving record checks because it is excluded from the public record.

This is why traffic school in California achieves virtually the same practical result as full dismissal, even though the ticket technically still exists.

## Does completing traffic school reset your point count to zero?

No. Point reduction removes a specific number of points. If you have 6 points and a course removes 4, you have 2 points remaining — not zero. The underlying violations that generated those points remain on your record.

## Does traffic school affect old points or only new ones?

It depends on the state:

- **[New York](/new-york) PIRP:** Only reduces points from violations that occurred within the 18 months before course completion.
- **[Georgia](/georgia):** Reduces active point total as of the completion date.
- **Michigan BDIC** (invitation-only, by Secretary of State letter): withholds points on the cited ticket; it does not dismiss the ticket or remove existing violations.

## Sources

The mechanism in each state cited above traces to its statute or DMV:

- **Texas** — Tex. Code Crim. Proc. Art. 45A.352 (driving safety course dismissal); Texas has had no points system since 2019 (HB 2048).
- **Arizona** — A.R.S. § 28-3392 (defensive driving diversion; citation dismissed). ([azcourts.gov](https://www.azcourts.gov/))
- **California** — Cal. Veh. Code § 1808.7 (conviction held confidential — "masked"). ([leginfo.legislature.ca.gov](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/))
- **Florida** — Fla. Stat. § 318.14(9), (11) (BDI; adjudication withheld, no points).
- **New York** — 15 NYCRR Parts 138 & 141 (PIRP; up to 4 points); NY Ins. Law § 2336 (10% discount). ([dmv.ny.gov](https://dmv.ny.gov/points-and-penalties/point-and-insurance-reduction-program))
- **Georgia** — O.C.G.A. § 40-5-86 (up to 7-point reduction). ([dds.georgia.gov](https://dds.georgia.gov/))
- **Virginia** — Va. Code §§ 46.2-489, 46.2-498 (driver improvement clinic; safe-driving points). ([dmv.virginia.gov](https://www.dmv.virginia.gov/))
- **Ohio** — O.R.C. § 4510.037 (two-point credit for an approved remedial course).
- **Wisconsin** — Wis. Admin. Code Trans 101.07 (3-demerit-point reduction, once every 3 years, via WisDOT-listed courses; no statewide dismissal). ([wisconsindot.gov](https://wisconsindot.gov/Pages/dmv/license-drvs/susp-or-rvkd/traf-safety.aspx))
- **Illinois** — court supervision defers judgment; there is no statewide point-reduction course (see [/illinois](/illinois)).
- **Michigan** — MCL 257.320d; Michigan SOS Basic Driver Improvement Course (invitation-only).
- **Tennessee** — Tenn. Dept. of Safety driver-improvement (points-removal) course; 4 hours, once every 4 years (see [/tennessee](/tennessee)).
